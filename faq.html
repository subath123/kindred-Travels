Traveler Directory FAQ

Your comprehensive guide to visa regulations, currency payments, local custom etiquette, and logistics in Sri Lanka.

Visa & Entry Requirements

Yes, all international travelers (except citizens of Singapore, Maldives, and Seychelles) must obtain an Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA) to enter Sri Lanka for tourism. You should apply online before you travel through the official government ETA portal. Applying in advance takes only 24–48 hours to process and helps you bypass the long "Visa on Arrival" queues at Bandaranaike International Airport in Colombo.
A standard tourist ETA is valid for 30 days from your date of entry and permits double-entry. The standard fee is $50 USD for most nationalities, while travelers from SAARC countries (South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation) pay a discounted rate of $20 USD. Children under the age of 12 are exempt from visa fees but still require a registered online ETA.
To enter Sri Lanka, your passport must be valid for at least 6 months beyond your entry date. Immigration officers at Colombo airport also regularly request proof of a confirmed return or onward flight ticket, as well as evidence of sufficient funds to cover your expenses during your stay.

Currency, Banking & Cash

The local currency is the Sri Lankan Rupee (LKR). Credit cards (predominantly Visa and MasterCard) are accepted at premium hotels, fine dining establishments, boutique stores, and supermarket chains in major areas like Colombo, Kandy, and Galle Fort. However, cash remains absolutely king for smaller purchases, local restaurants (kades), street vendors, three-wheelers (tuk-tuks), national park guides, and temple admission fees.
We highly recommend exchanging a small portion of your currency (major currencies like USD, EUR, GBP, AUD work best) at the 24/7 bank booths located in the arrival lobby of Colombo airport. For withdrawing cash, ATMs are widely available in cities and tourist towns. Banks like Commercial Bank, Bank of Ceylon (BOC), and Sampath Bank accept foreign cards. It is critical to alert your home bank of your travels to Sri Lanka to prevent foreign transaction blocks.

Safety & Local Etiquette

Sri Lanka is a very safe country for tourists. Local people are exceptionally hospitable and helpful. Violent crime against tourists is extremely rare. However, petty theft like bag snatching and pickpocketing can occur in crowded areas. For solo female travelers, it is safe, though standard precautions apply: avoid walking in unlit areas alone at night, ignore persistent beach touts, dress modestly when away from resort beaches, and use registered ride-hailing apps rather than unmetered street tuk-tuks. The dedicated Tourist Police emergency line is 1912.
When visiting Buddhist and Hindu temples, modesty is essential. Both men and women must cover their shoulders and knees. Slip-on shoes are ideal as you must remove footwear and headwear before entering temple grounds. Crucially, never pose for a photo with your back turned directly to a Buddha statue, as it is considered a severe sign of disrespect. Avoid pointing your feet at people or sacred items, and do not touch monks.

SIM Cards & Connectivity

Purchasing a local SIM card is very cheap and highly recommended. In the arrivals lobby of Bandaranaike International Airport in Colombo, there are dedicated booths for the major operators: Dialog Axiata and Mobitel. Dialog generally offers the best 4G LTE coverage and data speeds throughout the island, including deep inside the national parks and high-altitude tea hills. You will need your passport to register and purchase the card. A tourist pack containing 30GB to 50GB of data, local calls, and international credit costs approximately $10 to $15 USD.

Weather, Seasons & Monsoons

Sri Lanka experiences a tropical climate governed by two distinct monsoon systems, meaning there is always a sunny side of the island at any point in the year:
  • Southwest Monsoon (Yala): Brings rain to the south coast, west coast, and central highlands from May to September. During this time, the east coast (Trincomalee, Pasikudah) and north are dry, sunny, and perfect.
  • Northeast Monsoon (Maha): Brings rain to the north and east coasts from November to February. During this time, the south-western coast beaches, Kandy, and Ella are beautiful and sunny.
October and November are inter-monsoonal months, bringing unstable weather and frequent afternoon thunderstorms island-wide.

Transport, Trains & Getting Around

For short journeys within towns, local three-wheelers (tuk-tuks) are cheap and fun. We advise using ride-hailing apps like PickMe or Uber in Colombo, Kandy, and Galle to secure metered rates and avoid fare haggling. For long distances between cities, the most comfortable, stress-free, and common choice for boutique travelers is booking a private air-conditioned vehicle with a professional chauffeur-guide. This lets you stop at scenic spots at your own pace.
The mountain railway between Kandy, Nuwara Eliya (Nanu Oya), and Ella is celebrated as one of the world's most beautiful train journeys. Standard tickets for reserved seats (1st class observation deck, 2nd class, or 3rd class) open up exactly 30 days prior to the travel date. They sell out almost instantly. We highly recommend booking through a reputable travel operator (like Kindred Travels) or using the official Sri Lanka Railways ticketing portal ahead of time. Unreserved tickets can be bought at the station on the day of travel, but these carriages are extremely packed.

Payments, Prices & Tipping Etiquette

Tipping is a traditional and highly appreciated aspect of tourism in Sri Lanka. It serves as an acknowledgement of good service:
  • Restaurants: A 10% service charge is usually added, but an additional 5-10% in cash directly to your waiter is customary.
  • Private Drivers/Chauffeurs: We suggest tipping approximately $12 to $15 USD (equivalent in LKR) per day.
  • Safari Jeep Drivers & Guides: Tipping 1,500 to 2,000 LKR ($5 - $7 USD) is standard.
  • Hotel Porters: 100 to 200 LKR per bag.
Always keep small Rupee notes (100, 500 LKR) handy for tips, shoe-keepers at temples, and parking helpers.
